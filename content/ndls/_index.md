**A capability-focused dynamic bio-psycho-social modelling framework.**

NDLS treats neurodevelopmental conditions — ADHD, autism, Down syndrome,
learning disabilities — not as fixed labels but as dynamically interconnected
systems that take shape across a whole life. People are modelled as autonomous
agents whose biology, cognition and environment change each other over time,
rather than as cases to whom outcomes simply happen.

The framework brings two things together. Longitudinal causal analysis of
linked population data establishes what actually moves a trajectory;
agent-based simulation then lets those mechanisms play out across the settings
a life is lived in — individual, family, school, neighbourhood. Over the top
sits the capabilities approach as the normative layer, asking not only what
happens to people but what they are genuinely free to do and to be, and which
socio-economic resources and structural barriers widen or close that freedom.

The aim is twofold: to test causal hypotheses about how socio-educational
trajectories are made, and to give policy something better to aim at than a
diagnosis — capability-based interventions that can be simulated before they
are imposed.

## Why this framework

Most modelling of neurodevelopmental conditions is cross-sectional and
deficit-shaped: it measures what a person cannot do at one moment, against a
norm. That framing makes two mistakes at once. It freezes something that is
inherently developmental, and it locates the problem inside the person rather
than in the fit between a person and the settings they move through.

NDLS is an attempt to model the fit instead — which means modelling time,
environment and agency together, and being explicit that the thing worth
measuring is capability, not conformity.
